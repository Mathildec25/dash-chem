1 · Benchmarks: where they come from and why these three

A benchmark for this study must be a complete factorial grid (so the Pareto front is exact and a campaign is reproducible to the decimal), with named reagents (a chemist cannot argue with “L4”), and with room for a chemist to change the outcome — a reaction where plain BO always reaches the front measures nothing. The third criterion is read from the spread between seeds.

Two facts from the week that change how a result reads. The two Suzuki surrogates agree on the winning catalyst. Summit's own MLP emulators, evaluated on the same 5,670-point grid, disagree with Olympus by 8.8 yield points on average (up to 34.6, always at low temperature) but put the front on the same catalyst in all four cases — PCy3 for case II as in the paper. The trap is chemistry, not an artefact of one surrogate. The arylation has tied acquisition values. At experiment 30, 175 of the 1,698 remaining candidates share the acquisition maximum to the last digit (192 categorical cells, 17 visited), so the tail of an unaided campaign is an alphabetical sweep. Suzuki has no ties. The tie fraction is computable during a live campaign and says whether the optimiser is still choosing or merely enumerating.

3 · The trigger, frozen

The alert is your original signal with its denominator corrected — recent pace against the campaign's own average pace, both per experiment:

P* = (gain over the last W experiments / W) / (gain since the initial design / experiments since)

W = 5   threshold 0.30   cooldown 5   earliest alert: experiment 15 (= 10 + max(5 BO experiments, W))   no deadline

Why P* rather than P: under perfectly steady progress the original ratio decays like W/(t − ninit) whatever the campaign does, so its threshold silently encodes a firing time (0.10 fires at experiment 40 of a flawless campaign at budget 40, from experiment 40 onwards at budget 100). P* equals 1 under steady progress at any budget, so “0.30” means what it says. On the campaigns we hold the two are indistinguishable in detection; the threshold's portability decided it.

The pair (W, threshold) was chosen together, by measurement: at W = 5 a threshold of 0.10 leaves four campaigns silent, two of them failures; 0.30 alerts 13 of 13 failed case II campaigns at 2.5 solicitations each, first alert at 22.6 with 26 % of the final gain still to come — and the one campaign never interrupted is the one that reaches 100 %. W = 3 / 0.10 detects three experiments earlier (35 % of the gain left) at 3.1 solicitations; W = 5 was adopted for the load on five chemists. Cooldown 3 or 5 changes nothing to the first alert, only how often the chemist is disturbed afterwards.

What the chemist sees at an alert. The campaign pauses. Three answers: let the optimiser continue (its next proposal is shown), propose the next experiment yourself, or stop the campaign. Each answer is recorded with its moment, P*, the optimiser's proposal, and a free-text justification.

4 · Reproducibility, which blocked everything

Two identical runs of one campaign diverged from experiment 26. Cause: REACTO built the BoFire MoboStrategy without a seed, and BoFire then draws one from np.random.SeedSequence() — operating-system entropy, unreachable by torch.manual_seed or np.random.seed — at every one of the thirty acquisition steps. Where two candidates were nearly tied, the argmax flipped.

Fix: bayesian_optimization(..., seed=None) now passes a seed to the strategy when given (the application is unchanged bit for bit when it is not). The bench derives it arithmetically from the campaign seed and the iteration, 100000 + 1000·seed + iteration, so it can be recovered by hand from a log. Verified: two runs coincide on all 40 experiments and to the twelfth decimal on the area under the curve; a fork without intervention reproduces its parent exactly at branch points 15, 22 and 30. The 51 earlier campaigns stay valid as independent samples but cannot serve as controls; everything below was recomputed.

5 · The random-intervention arm: the null model, measured

Design, as agreed: three arms sharing their history up to the alert — plain BO (control), a random untested grid point injected at every alert (the null), a chemist's point at every alert (the human study). The injected point replaces the optimiser's proposal, so every arm spends exactly 40 experiments; alerts are recomputed on the branch after each injection; each control is branched several times with different draws.

C–H arylationCampaignsMean fraction of the frontReach 90 %Experiments to 90 % (reachers)
Plain BO200.88810 (50 %)26.2

Paired gain +1.3 points of the front; 18 of 40 branches improve, Wilson 95 % [31 %, 60 %]; range −26.7 to +23.1. A random point at the alerts moves a campaign a lot, in both directions, and on average almost not at all. That is the good outcome for the study: if the chemists beat this, it is expertise, not the effect of an exploratory shake. The little gain there is comes from luck on the catalyst: 25 of the 130 injected points carried a front ligand (19 %) and those gained +3.2 versus +0.9 — the quantity a chemist, who does not pick a ligand at random, has to beat.

The most important warning of the week. The estimate of the random arm's gain collapsed as the sample grew: +4.0 points on 11 branches, +2.7 on 19, +1.3 on 40. This is small-sample optimism in its plainest form, and exactly the regime of a human arm with five chemists and a few answers each. Do not conclude on a mean over a dozen answers; compare paired, on the same checkpoints, against the random arm.

6 · The initial design decides — and that frames the human study

Every campaign is seeded by one integer that fixes both the Latin hypercube and the acquisition's Monte-Carlo samples, so “seed 2 is trapped” did not say which of the two was responsible. With the acquisition now seedable, the test was run: same initial design, four acquisition seeds.

CampaignInitial design heldBO seeds triedOutcome
Arylation, seed 2seed 2472.5 % of the front, all four (only the tie-sweep tail differs)
Suzuki II, seed 3seed 3 (PCy3 drawn twice)437.3 %, all four; one visit to PCy3 in thirty experiments every time

Given this optimiser, the outcome is a function of the ten starting points; the acquisition's own randomness contributes nothing. It does not say another optimiser would be trapped by the same design.

Frame for the article (your decision of 12 September). The human study is a case study on trapped initial designs: the base rate comes from the twenty seeds (13/20, 5/20, 10/20); on one trapped design per reaction, three paired branches share the history up to the alert — plain BO (deterministic given the design), a random point, a chemist. The question is precise: does a chemist called between experiments 15 and 22 get the campaign out of a trap that neither the optimiser nor chance leaves? Not “does HITL help on average”, which the sample size cannot carry.

7 · The live page in REACTO

/hitl-live replaces both the notebook and the static checkpoint page. The campaign is a state machine in hitl_bench/live.py, tested without any interface; the Dash page only draws it.

  • Identical for everyone until they act. A chemist's campaign replays the assigned control — same initial design, same proposals in the same order — at one experiment per second. The moment they impose a point, the optimiser runs for real from there, seeded as the control was, so even the diverged part is replayable.
  • The alert is the frozen trigger, evaluated on the live history exactly as on the random arm. The campaign pauses; the three answers are recorded with P*, the optimiser's proposal, timing and justification. A missing condition is refused, a blank choice is refused.
  • Saved after every experiment, as a REACTO project (opens in Results like any other) and as a JSON carrying what Excel cannot.
  • Chemistry first. A collapsible card shows the source figure — your four-case scheme for Suzuki, Fig. 4a of Shields et al. for the arylation — one sentence, conditions, what is controlled and measured, catalogue prices per gram behind the cost objective, sources. The experiments table (sortable, as in Results) comes right after; plots last. No hypervolume, no acquisition, no front, no published optimum.
  • Resume as on the Optimization page: a returning chemist picks their name in a list; each campaign shows not started / in progress n/40 / paused at experiment k — waiting for you / finished. Names are case-insensitive.
  • What the browser taught us. The whole callback chain passed over HTTP, and “Open” still did nothing in Chrome: the Dash renderer refuses a callback whose plain-id input is absent from the page. Fixed with pattern ids. A per-campaign lock keeps two ticks from stepping the same campaign during a 10-second live experiment. The Suzuki catalyst menus showed codes while the table showed names; both now go through one label, and the round trip is checked.
  • L6 and L7 have names. Your Scheme names them: PPh3 and P(t-Bu)3; the text of the article names only L1–L5. Chemists no longer see “P1-L7”.

Your own trial of the three campaigns (as “mc”) exercised every path: continue at four alerts on Suzuki II and on the arylation, an imposed point at 23 and a stop at 29 on Suzuki I (43.5 % against 48.0 % for plain BO at the same experiment). scripts/collect_live.py turns every chemist's files into two tables, one row per campaign (paired against the control at the same experiment and at the full budget) and one per decision.
